Genomic sequencing has revolutionized cancer treatment. What was once thought to be a uniform and had a singular "cure" is now being treated case by case. 
Personalized medicine or precision medicine has been radical in cancer treatment. Genomic sequencing in tumors has allowed oncologists and researchers see that 
mutations or genetic alterations can greatly contribute to tumors/cancer. An SNP or Single Nucleotide Polymorphism is a common genetic variation where 
a singular building block of DNA is different in an individual's genome. SNPs are normal and usually inherited rom parents.
However, when tumors or cancers are involved the SNP plays a huge role. SNPs are linked to inherited susceptibility of cancer and changes in the body 
because of the tumor; SNPs modify gene expression and protein function among other things.
Driver genes are the modifictions made that allow growth advantaged for selected cells. This can "drive" and directly progress the spread of a tumor.
Oncogenes usually help to control growth of cells, but some mutations can cause them to divide cells rapidly and uncontrollably.
Tumor supressor genes usually are the insurance to the body. They oversee cell division, DNA repair, and apoptosis, but deactivation from select mutations can 
eradicate these safety genes.

It is very important to note. Not all mutations are dangerous cancerous mutations. Passenger mutations are mutations that do not directly contribute to the 
progression of the tumor but are "passengers". The typical tumor only needs about two to eight driver genes mutations to develop to its full potential. The rest 
of the mutations are passenger. In 2013, 140 genes were found that develop and drive cancer. This finding has allowed breakthrough to indentifying what specifically
changes in genes to develop tumors. Driver genes cand categorized into twelve signaling pathways that regulate three core cellular processes. They are cell fate, 
cell survival, and genome maintenance. 
Cell fate is a cell's outcome or specilizations it takes on in its life.
Cell survival is a cell's ability to stay living and maintain metabolic processes.
Genomic maintenance is the system of cellular proceeses that protects genetic material, preservse accurate DNA replication, and prevents genetic mutations.
All in all, genomic sequencing has revealed the "genomic landscape" of prevailing "human" cancers. 
For most cancer types, there are "mountains" and "hills".
Mountains are small in number and are genes doctored in a high percentage of tumors.
Hills are much larger in number and are genes altered infrequently. 
Therefore, different people means different cancer. Two people may have the same cancer type but radically different mutations.
This is reinforced by many other breakthrough findings. 
Genomic sequencing has fundaentally changed cancer research. However, finding the mutation is only the first step. Researchers and scientists must identify 
driver and passenger mutations to effectivelt understand and treat tumors/cancers.
In conclusion, tumors and cancers are diseases of the genome, and understanding/data of the genome are the key to treatment and complete comprehension. 
Sources: (Vogelstein et. al, 2013) "Cancer Genome Landscapes" and (Garraway and Lander, 2013) "Lessons from the Cancer Genome"
