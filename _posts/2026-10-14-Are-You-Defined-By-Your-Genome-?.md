The question, “Are you defined by your genome?” does not have a simple yes or no answer. 
The genome is essentially the instructions for living organisms. Cells use the instructions given to 
keep the organism alive, to keep the organism growing, to keep the organism maintained. The genome seems to 
define an organism. But, another important factor in an organism’s life is their environment and circumstance. 
Advantageous genes and a non-advantageous circumstances do not always yield results and achievements of full potential. 

According to the European Molecular Biology Laboratory, Genes are only a factor. 
For example, a person born with advantageous genes for intelligence, but they grew up in a poor school district. 
A district that could not afford to properly educate all their students. The person may never go to college or read proficiently. 
Because, their nurture and environment was not on par with their genes.

The importance and impact of the genome is not to be understated. Predispositions to diseases, physical traits, and
even mental disorders cannot be avoided by good diet and/or optimal climate. 
The genome does not define, doom, or save an individual. Each organism has a unique genome as well as a unique set of 
life circumstances. The combination of environment with genome defines a livelihood. 

In conclusion, multiple factors contribute to a living organism. There is not one thing or one trait or one circumstance
that has the ability to define an organism and its life. The genome is a major driving force of lives. It is essentially 
a cellular blueprint, and our environment, nurture, and circumstances also are a guide to our lives.
